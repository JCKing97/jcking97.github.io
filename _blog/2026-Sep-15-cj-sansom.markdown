I find it hard to summarise the impact of C. J.'s writing on me. For over a decade I explored the world and characters that he infused with life. His writing helped rekindle my love of reading. His weaving of history into a narrative sparked a love of history and his thrilling criminal investigations drew me ever deeper into the characters' lives.<br/><br/>

He is best known for his Shardlake series. It brings to life the life and events of Tudor England in a way that made it so relatable and thrilling that I had not come across something like it before. Dissolution went underrated by me for a long time, but it's quiet studied exploration of a Tudor monastery was the right introduction for his understated characters. Revelation bought the thriller side of the series to a peak, diving into a religious madness.<br/><br/>

But it was his last book in the series, Tombland, that he took bold steps beyond the bounds of crime-thriller history genre. Here readers lived in the camp of Kett's rebellion. It's slow exploration of daily life in the camp contrasted against the charged intensity of lives upended by the cruelty of those in power and enabled the characters to deepen further even after 7 books of development. It also gave the reader so much time to drink deeply the experience of the people and this is what I'm truly thankful for.<br/><br/>

Beyond the Shardlake series, he wrote and released an alternative Second World War historical fiction, Dominion, and a historical fiction set in the Spanish Civil War, Winter in Madrid. It is the latter that truly captured my attention time and time again. It is less a book about war and more an exploration of how people who experienced this tragic war have been forgotten. It is amazing to me to think that George Orwell and Ernest Hemingway wrote so enigmatically about it and yet it has been almost ignored in popular culture.<br/><br/>

The Spanish Civil War doesn't have the clean-cut nature of good triumphing over evil that the Second World War has. An evil fascist dictatorship certainly triumphed, but what would have happened if the Stalin-backed political left had won? Besides the west colluded with Francoist spain until the seventies. So it serves no political side to sponsor it's history. But this is precisely what makes it so interesting. It is a mostly grey human experience based in realities which C. J. captured through his grounded writing without flowery description and his story which though gripping is without the traditional thrill of a spy novel. This might not wholly sound like a compliment, but in reality, it is his nuance and relatability that made his novels excellent.<br/><br/>

C. J. sadly passed away in 2024. But, he has left a collection of novels that have enriched so many people's lives including my won. I am so grateful for his contribution to the world and for having the chance to experience what he has crafted.
