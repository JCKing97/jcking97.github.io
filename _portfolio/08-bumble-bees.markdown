When you think of pollinators, what animal comes to mind first? For most people it's bees. Of course, entemologists want us all to know how flowering plants have all evolved to work with a variety of insects. And they're right. But not much captures the imagination of people as much as the emblematic colours and buzz of a bee.<br/><br/>

The first sight of a bumble bee in the year is always exciting. Different species emerge at different times and the Early Bumble Bee is even named for beginning to found it's colony as early as February. This picture was taken at Michelham Priory at the colony peak in the height of summer. One of the key identifying features on this is the slightly darker abdomen tip, giving it the name the <a href="https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/400282763"><span class="species-name">Buff-tailed Bumble Bee<i class="fa-solid fa-dove" style="color: #CD7F32;" aria-hidden="true"></i></span></a>.<br/><br/>

You can see this individual foraging in the flowers for nectar and pollen. They are a social species of bee, with one queen per colony, a sterile group of female workers and a set of male drones whose sole responsibility is leave the nest and mate. It is likely based on size (slightly smaller) and the buff-tail (workers have a whiter tail) that this is a male drone.
