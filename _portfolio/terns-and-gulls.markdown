These seabirds make up the Laridae family. Gulls are well know to all (even if there is no single seagull), but terns are less present in our lives. They often require far more secluded shingle in order to reproduce.<br/><br/>

It is a diverse family including the bird with the longest migration each year the Arctic Tern, the city dwelling Herring Gull and the purely maritime Kittiwake.<br/><br/>

Here a <span class="species-name">Common Tern</span> is pictured at Rye Harbour Nature Reserve. A number of species of tern nest on the shingle of the islands at the reserve including Common, Sandwich and Little Terns.
