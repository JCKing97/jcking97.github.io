Is there a more transformative group of life than trees? They have so drastically changed our world as to make it liveable for large animals. They are not only a rich resource for making our homes and furniture but also have fed our ancestors for as long as we have been around.<br/><br/>

More recently, they have been identified as key for combatting climate change and for combatting the anxiety that this trend is bringing into our lives. There are not many more calming experiences than sitting in the sun dappled shade of a beech woodland or foraging for sweet chestnuts in the autumn.<br/><br/>

Many species of this tree are so recognisable by their distinctive leaves. The Oak is both the National Trust and Woodland Trusts's logos, the Ash is made up of complex thin leaflets and the Hawthorn's lobes identify it as a great spring salad leaf. Pictured is a <span class="species-name">Common Alder</span> leaf.<br/><br/>

Alder is a slightly lesser known tree that oddly produces cone-like structures for reproduction and loves moist ground. Unlike others it will stand strong against rot providing excellent nest sites for otters.
